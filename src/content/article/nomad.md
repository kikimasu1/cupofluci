I had an EVO ICL eye surgery in Tokyo in late October. To put it simply, a certified eye doctor put two *contact lenses* inside your eyes. This special kind of contacts is only made by a Swiss company.

I had the surgery for around 3.4k USD. This is a pro of living in Japan, because the same surgery costs around 10,000 USD in the States.

I wanted (and still want) to be a digital nomad, and I'm experiencing this kind of lifestyle. Not until I was actually living the life did I discover some inconveniences.

I used to travel to Europe a lot when I was an undergrad in the UK. At that time I thought if I had a fully remote job, I would travel often after getting my work done — it'd be great.

My 2 internships in the UK allowed me to work fully remote. However, I felt kinda bored living like that. The fully remote job in the States also made me feel kinda bored — I moved to a low-cost-of-living state, only to find out there aren't that many things to do.

I can benefit from different systems by being a digital nomad. Getting medical treatments in Japan is cheap cuz they have national health insurance and I only need to pay 30% for basically everything. Let alone the things I can do & the great public transportation.

I used to think if I could live off being a part-time worker I would do so. Now I'm thinking maybe I should (or at least try to) get a full-time job wherever I move, cuz it comes with pension & healthcare insurance. Also the skills are transferable & can be put on a resume. Side hustles' income is unstable & usually don't come w/ any insurance.

International cities usually have more possibilities for getting a job. It's for the best for me to stay at big companies or startups — it'll be easier if I want to change jobs too.

To thrive in a new city, you need savings that allow you to not work for a long time, and it's better to have a side hustle w/ stable income. It'll be better if you can find a job beforehand. It's hard to tell the job market before you move there.

One needs different support systems in life. Socializing with others is just one of them. You need to build your own support system.

Getting new housing / buying furniture / paying medical & social insurance / figuring out local policies is more tiring than I expected. I feel like it takes me at least 3 months to live comfortably in one place. I'm planning on staying in one place for 1-2 years as a start. If I like it I'll stay longer; if not I'll just leave.

By living like this, my mentality had a great shift. I'm much more open-minded and love & peace coded. I hope everyone can live their best lives so that the world will be a better place.

I also became more proactive, because I have to fight for myself every time. By interacting with the world, I'm figuring out who I am as a person and what I want.

I'm learning from doing, cuz it's my action that reveals my true values. I'm the maker of my future.
